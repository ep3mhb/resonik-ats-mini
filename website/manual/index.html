Using the RESONIK additions

Full user manual (PDF, A4) · راهنمای کاربری فارسی (PDF، A4)

This page covers what RESONIK adds. Everything else — bands, modes, memories, seek and scan, EiBi schedules, RDS, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth — works as in the official firmware and is described in the ats-mini user manual.

The basics: rotate the encoder to tune. A click (under half a second) opens the Menu and selects; a short press (over half a second) opens the volume, or does the item's second action; a long press (two seconds) turns the display off and on. Menu paths on this page are for the tile menu, the default from v2.45-r2; see The menu for where each item sits, and for the classic menu.

CW mode

Choose Menu → Tuning → Mode → CW (it follows AM). The display shows the frequency of the CW signal itself, the way a CW transceiver does: tune until the frequency matches a spot or a schedule and the tone is in the middle of the filter.

BandwidthToneUse
0.5k (default)500 HzCrowded bands, weak signals
1.0k1000 HzFinding signals, faster tuning
1.2k and wider700 HzListening around the band

The step starts at 10 Hz; change it in Menu → Tuning → Step. CW uses the USB calibration value of the band (Menu → Ham & SWL → Calibration).

Band plan

On the amateur bands from 160 to 10 m, a coloured strip under the tuning scale shows the IARU Region 1 band plan, and the segment you are in is named next to the pointer.

ColourSegment
CyanCW only
YellowNarrow modes: CW and digital (FT8, PSK, RTTY…)
RedInternational beacons (NCDXF/IARU)
GreenAll modes, including SSB phone
GreySatellite and FM (10 m)

Menu → Ham & SWL → Band Plan:

  • Off — no strip.
  • Scale — the strip and segment name only.
  • Auto (default) — tuning into a segment also sets the mode, filter and step for it: CW with a narrow filter in the CW part, USB for digital and beacons, LSB below 10 MHz and USB above in the phone part. A short message confirms it. If you then change the mode yourself, your choice stays until you leave the segment. In AM it switches only when you tune into an amateur band from outside; AM chosen by hand inside a band stays.

Menu → Ham & SWL → Plan Area r3 chooses which plan is drawn:

The Region 2 and 3 plans are simplified guidance. National allocations differ; check your own licence before transmitting with another radio.

Time zone and dual clock

Menu → System → Time Zone lists UTC first, then every UTC offset with named places beside it — Dubai and Muscat next to UTC+4, London, Berlin/Paris, New York, Sydney and others. Named places change to summer time and back automatically. A previous offset setting is carried over.

When the zone is not UTC, the time line in the info box alternates every few seconds between local date and time and UTC: time — the time used by schedules, logs and beacon timetables. Time: --:-- means the clock is not set yet.

Menu → System → Date/Time shows the running time in UTC. Rotate to change a value, click for the next field, and short-press (over half a second) to save. Leaving without a change leaves the clock alone.

Setting the clock

The radio has no clock battery, so the time is lost when it is switched off. To have it set automatically at every power-on:

  1. Add your Wi-Fi network (see the ats-mini manual, Wi-Fi).
  2. Set Menu → System → Wi-Fi → Sync Only. The radio connects, sets the time and switches Wi-Fi off again to save battery. (Connect mode also sets the time, and stays connected.)

Messages: Time synchronized (internet time server), Time set from web (the network blocks time servers; the time was read from a web server instead), or Time sync failed with a second line giving the reason — show it to whoever looks after your network. Without Wi-Fi, an FM station sending RDS time also sets the clock, or use the web settings page: Use Browser Date/Time copies your phone's or computer's time and time zone.

Battery time

On battery, the voltage at the top right alternates with an estimate of the listening time left, for example ~8h20. The ~ means a typical consumption is assumed; after about ten minutes the radio uses its own measured drain and the ~ disappears. Screen brightness, volume and Wi-Fi all change the result. On USB power the charging symbol is shown instead.

Menu → Display → Battery (v2.46-r5) chooses what is shown beside the battery icon: Volts + time (as above), Volts, Percent or Icon only.

Automatic attenuation

In AM with Menu → Audio → AGC/ATTN set to On, a very strong signal — a local medium-wave station, a big broadcaster at night — can overload the receiver and cause distortion and stations that should not be there. The radio now adds attenuation in steps when that happens and removes it again on weaker stations. The info box shows Att: A12 while it is active (A for automatic). Turn it off in Menu → Audio → Auto Att., or set AGC/ATTN by hand, which always takes over.

The menu v2.45-r2

A click opens the menu on six categories of large tiles. Turn the encoder to move the highlight and click to open a category. Inside, the first tile is Back; a category with more than five items has a second page (the dots at the bottom show which page you are on). A short press closes the menu from anywhere.

Listening controls such as Band, Mode, Step or Volume open their list as before; after your choice the menu closes, so the encoder tunes straight away. Settings open full screen as a list of values, or a number with a bar; a click keeps the value and returns to the category.

Menu → System → Knob: 20 clicks or 30 clicks per turn, for the two kinds of encoder fitted to these radios. If the knob skips a step, or needs two clicks for one, choose the other value (the web page calls it Half-step Encoder).

From v2.46-r5 there are two more: Enc. Accel. in System (the knob speeds up when turned quickly) and Capacitor in Tuning (the antenna capacitor on medium wave). Each has its own section below.

Menu → Display → Menu Style → Classic brings back the small list menu of the official firmware. From v2.45-r4 it has the same six groups as the tiles: choose a group and click; the first row of each group is < Back, and the box title names the group. After a setting is changed the menu returns to its group. Tiles switches back.

Beacons v2.45-r2

Eighteen beacons of the NCDXF/IARU International Beacon Project take turns on 14100, 18110, 21150, 24930 and 28200 kHz, ten seconds each, and repeat every three minutes. Each sends its callsign in CW and then four dashes at 100 W, 10 W, 1 W and 0.1 W, so how many dashes you hear tells you how good the path is.

Tune to one of the five frequencies (CW or USB): the station line shows the beacon on the air now and the seconds left, for example Beacon 4S7B Sri Lanka 6s. The clock must be set.

Menu → Ham & SWL → Beacons and a callsign makes the radio follow that beacon: it changes band every ten seconds as the beacon moves from 20 m to 10 m. A beacon is on the air for 50 seconds of every three minutes; while it is silent the radio waits on 14.100 MHz in CW, where the next run begins (v2.46-r5: it goes there as soon as following starts, so you see at once that it has begun, and the beacon's first slot is heard in full). Following ends when you tune by hand, choose another band or switch to FM (from v2.46-r5 also while the radio is waiting for the beacon), and the radio stays where you went. Choosing Off or Names only in the list, or Stop in Web Control, ends it too and takes the radio back to the band, frequency and mode it was on before following began (v2.46-r5). In Web Control, the top of the Beacons tab has Follow a beacon: choose the beacon, press Start, and Stop to end it. The line beside the buttons says which beacon is being followed, the frequency it is on now or the seconds to its next turn, and why following did not start or ended (the clock is not set, you tuned away). Tapping a beacon on the map starts it too.

The first two choices of the list (v2.45-r4): Off also hides the beacon names, and Names only names the beacon on the air without following one. The choice is kept across restarts. The Web Control page has a live world map of the beacons (Web Control).

Memory scan v2.45-r2

Menu → Memory → Mem Scan steps through the stored memories (at least two) and holds on any with a signal; it carries on four seconds after the signal goes. A signal means the mode's squelch level if you have set one, otherwise an SNR of 6 dB or more. Rotate the encoder or click to stop: the radio stays on that memory.

Signal logger v2.45-r2

Menu → Memory → Logger writes a line every 10 seconds, minute or five minutes: UTC time, frequency, mode, band, signal strength (dBµV), SNR (dB) and the station name. Leave the radio on a frequency overnight to see when a path opens and closes.

Download the file from the radio's web page, Log (CSV), and open it in a spreadsheet. Clear log in the same menu (click to confirm) deletes it. Logging stops by itself at 1 MB.

Propagation v2.45-r2

Menu → Ham & SWL → Propagation shows the solar flux (SFI), sunspot number, A and K index, X-ray level and the day and night conditions for 80–40, 30–20, 17–15 and 12–10 m, as published by N0NBH on hamqsl.com. The data is fetched whenever Wi-Fi is up — including the time Sync at power-on — and at most every 15 minutes. Higher SFI and low K mean better conditions on the higher bands.

EiBi schedules v2.45-r2

Menu → Ham & SWL → Load EiBi now loads the season's schedule with its days of the week, so a station that is on air only at weekends is shown only then, and adds the language and target area after the name, for example BBC World Service [E > ME] (English, to the Middle East). After updating from v2.45-r1, load the schedule once more.

CAT control v2.45-r2

Menu → System → USB Port → CAT TS-480 makes the radio answer Kenwood TS-480 commands on its USB port, so programs can read and set frequency and mode and read the S-meter. In the program choose Kenwood TS-480 (Hamlib rig 2028) on the radio's serial port, with split and CAT PTT off; the speed setting does not matter. Transmit commands are refused: this is a receiver. Switch back to Ad hoc for the ats-mini remote tools and screenshots.

From v2.46-r5 the choice is called CAT TS-480 (it was CAT) and is also on two more ports: TCP Port → CAT TS-480 for a program on the same Wi-Fi network (the radio's address, port 60000), and Bluetooth → CAT TS-480, both in the same System group. New with it are VFO A and B, squelch, steps up and down and automatic status reports; the CW mode is mode 3, as on the TS-480. A program that asks for the frequency every second does not stop a seek or a scan; its answers come when the seek ends.

An example: flrig

flrig is a free rig-control program for macOS, Windows and Linux, from w1hkj.org; this was tested with version 2.0.12 on a Mac.

  1. On the radio choose USB Port → CAT TS-480 and plug in the cable.
  2. In flrig open Config → Setup → Xcvr. Rig: TS-480SAT. Port: the radio's serial port; on a Mac its name begins with /dev/cu.usbmodem. Baud: any, for example 115200. Leave RTS/CTS, RTS +12 v and DTR +12 v unticked, and press Init.
  3. Under Poll tick S-mtr, or flrig's S-meter stays empty.
  4. Under Restore untick the boxes if the radio should keep what you set from the program. With them ticked, flrig puts the radio back as it found it when it quits.

Over Wi-Fi instead of the cable: on the radio TCP Port → CAT TS-480; in flrig Config → Setup → TCPIP & TCI enter the radio's address and port 60000 and tick Use tcpip. One program at a time can be connected; Web Control can be open in a browser as well.

What you will see

  • The program follows the knob, and sets frequency, mode and volume; Tune and transmit do nothing. flrig's volume is in percent: 50 there is 31 on the radio's scale of 63.
  • flrig shows nothing above 99.999 MHz, because a TS-480 ends at 60 MHz: the upper FM band and memories there are not shown. The radio itself works as usual. Other programs may stop at 60 MHz.
  • During Seek and Scan the program stands still: the radio answers when the search is over. Auto Scan and Mem Scan are followed as they go.
  • A program connected over Wi-Fi keeps Wi-Fi on while the FT8, CW or Digital page is open, and follows the frequency the page tunes to.
  • Quitting the program leaves the radio as it is. If the cable is pulled while flrig runs, flrig stops answering: quit and reopen it.

FT8 and FT4 decoder v2.45-r3

Menu → Ham & SWL → FT8/FT4 opens a full-screen page that decodes FT8 from the radio's own audio. It tunes the FT8 frequency of the band you are on (or 40 m) in USB with the 3.0 kHz filter. Under the header a waterfall scrolls; after each 15 second slot the messages appear with signal report (dB), audio frequency (Hz) and text, CQ calls in green.

  • Turn the encoder to change band: 160, 80, 40, 30, 20, 17, 15, 12 and 10 m, then the FT4 frequencies of 80 to 10 m (FT4 in yellow, 7.5 second slots).
  • Click clears the list; a short press leaves and puts the radio back exactly as it was.
  • With the clock set (Wi-Fi time sync) it decodes from the first slot. Without a clock it still decodes: the header says No clock: syncing while it finds the slot timing from the stations it hears, usually within a minute or two, then Synced to band.
  • The page takes in the radio's sound up to 3 kHz, which is all FT8 and FT4 use; see Decoder Audio for the 6 kHz choice.
  • Bottom left, In is the audio level reaching the decoder (10 to 150 is fine), and Last the number of messages in the last slot.

The page pauses Wi-Fi while it is open. It needs the V4B board, which wires the speaker line to the processor (which board do I have?); on other boards it shows No audio on the decoder input. On boards that take the audio after the speaker amplifier the speaker stays on (Spk next to the level).

FT8 signals are often 15 to 20 dB below the noise. An indoor whip hears few of them: a window, a balcony or a few metres of wire on the antenna make a big difference, and so does keeping chargers and LED lamps away.

CW decoder v2.45-r3

Menu → Ham & SWL → CW Decoder turns Morse into text on a full-screen page, with the speed (WPM), tone, a level bar and a key indicator. Tune so the tone sits at the pitch of the CW filter; turn to tune, click to clear the text, short press to leave. The speed follows the sender by itself from about 5 to 40 WPM. Static crashes and clicks are ignored; letters it cannot read are shown as *. While the page is open the radio plays at least at a fixed level, so the decoder hears the same whatever the volume; it is never quieter than your own volume (v2.46-r4).

Menu → Ham & SWL → CW Strip → On shows the decoded text along the bottom of the normal screen while in CW mode. Like the FT8 page it needs the V4B board, and it works while Wi-Fi is off.

Setup at first start v2.46-r5

A newly installed radio, or one after a factory reset, asks four things in turn: the time zone, the knob (20 or 30 clicks per turn), the band plan for your licence, and whether to set up Wi-Fi now. Turn to choose, click for the next, short press to skip. Everything can be changed later in the menu. To see it again, do a factory reset: switch the radio off, hold the knob pressed, switch it on and keep holding until Resetting Preferences shows. The reset erases the settings, memories, Wi-Fi networks and the files on the radio (schedule, signal log, start picture, DSP patches); save a backup first on the Config web page and restore it afterwards.

Freq Entry, Band View and Auto-Store v2.46-r5

Menu → Tuning → Freq Entry is a number pad to go straight to a frequency. Turn the knob to move over the keys and click to press one: type the number, then kHz or MHz. So 14074 and kHz, or 14.074 and MHz, both tune 14.074 MHz. When the frequency is outside the band you are on, the radio changes band, and goes to or from FM as needed. Del removes the last digit, Clear all of them, and Back or a short press leaves without tuning. It takes 150 kHz to 30 MHz and 64 to 108 MHz.

Menu → Tuning → Band View sweeps 160 frequencies around the one you are on, again and again. The top of the page is the sweep in progress as bars, filled in from the left as it goes, with the sweep before it dimmer ahead of it; under it the finished sweeps form a waterfall, the newest on top. Weak signals are blue, strong ones yellow. The line under the title gives the frequencies the view starts and ends at; the title line gives the frequency under the cursor. The sound is off meanwhile. A sweep takes some seconds, since the receiver has to settle on every frequency. Turn to move the cursor, click to tune there, short press to leave. In Web Control, Band View in the Band scope box starts the same on the radio and shows it in the browser; click a peak to tune there, which ends it, or press Stop. Menu → Memory → Auto-Store sweeps once and puts the strongest stations in free memory slots, named Auto; stations already stored are skipped.

Decodes in the browser v2.46-r5

Open Web Control and its Decodes tab, then the FT8/FT4, CW Decoder or Digital page on the radio: Wi-Fi stays on while Web Control is open. The tab shows the band as a waterfall, every FT8 and FT4 message with the station's country and flag, a map of the stations that sent a locator (type your own locator to see lines from you), the text of CW, and the whole text of RTTY, NAVTEX and PSK31 in a box that scrolls. Type callsigns or countries in DX alarm to hear a tone when one is decoded; Save log downloads the list.

The row Decoder on the radio starts and stops the decoders without touching the radio. Choose FT8 or FT4 and a band, or CW, RTTY, RTTY DWD, NAVTEX, PSK31 or WEFAX, and press Start. FT8 and FT4 go to the band you choose and NAVTEX to the channel you choose in the second list. For RTTY, RTTY DWD, PSK31 and WEFAX the second list holds frequencies where each is heard (the German weather service, Northwood, the RTTY and PSK31 parts of the bands), or As tuned to stay where the radio is; CW decodes where the radio is. While CW or a Digital mode runs, the row under the buttons shows the radio's frequency: ◀ ▶ act as the radio's knob (they tune, or step the NAVTEX channel) and Tune goes to the frequency you type, staying in USB. Stop closes the decoder and the radio goes back to where it was. The line beside the buttons says what is running.

The whole text, and saving it

For RTTY, NAVTEX and PSK31 the box under the waterfall holds the whole text the radio has decoded, with its line breaks, and follows it as it grows. Scroll up to read; it stays where you leave it. The browser fetches from the radio whatever it has not got yet, so text decoded before you opened the page is there too. Save text downloads it as a text file; Clear text empties the box on this page only. The two red lines on the waterfall are the tones the decoder listens to, and sync beside the mode means NAVTEX is locked to the station.

NAVTEX timetable

Choose NAVTEX and a timetable opens under the buttons, for the channel in the second list. Every NAVTEX station sends for ten minutes every four hours, at the turn its letter gives it: A at 00:00 UTC, B at 00:10, and so on to X at 03:50. The same letter opens each of its messages: ZCZC FA12 comes from station F.

  • now, in green, marks a station that is on the air, with the minutes it has left. The others follow in the order they come.
  • Type My locator and the table shows the distance to each station. Order then sorts by time or Nearest first, and Range keeps the stations within 1500, 3000 or 6000 km.
  • A click on a line starts NAVTEX on that channel. Show all opens the whole list.
  • 518 kHz is in English, 490 kHz in the country's own language, 4209.5 kHz reaches far on short wave. Night is best on 518 and 490 kHz. The eight short-wave channels further down the list carry safety information for the high seas; few stations send there and they have no timetable.

The table holds 238 stations, compared on 22 Nov 2026 with Alan Gale G4TMV's World NAVTEX Database for DXers (ndblist.info), Wikipedia's List of Navtex stations and the station file of fldigi. Stations change, and a decoder on a pocket radio is not for navigation.

RTTY, NAVTEX, PSK31 and WEFAX v2.46-r5

Menu → Ham & SWL → Digital decodes RTTY (amateur, 45.45 baud and 170 Hz shift), RTTY DWD (the German weather service, 50 baud and 450 Hz), NAVTEX (maritime safety messages on 518, 490 and 4209.5 kHz and on eight short-wave channels for the high seas), PSK31 and WEFAX weather charts (tune 1.9 kHz below the published frequency; the chart is drawn on the screen and, larger, in the browser). The page switches the radio to USB with the 3.0k filter and shows the band's spectrum with the tones it listens to; it finds the signal by itself, so tune until it is in the picture. Click for the next mode, turn to tune (in NAVTEX the knob steps through the channels, and the page tunes each by itself), short press to leave. Each mode keeps its own frequency until the radio is switched off; the first time they start on RTTY 14080, RTTY DWD 10099.3, PSK31 14070 and WEFAX 8038.1 kHz. Opened on short or medium wave, the page stays where the radio is; it never works on FM. The screen shows the last five lines; the radio keeps the whole text, about 16 000 characters: push the knob and turn it to read it back seven lines at a time, then turn scrolls and a click returns to the live text. Each start of a decoder writes a head line with the mode, the frequency and the UTC time. Like the FT8 page it needs the V4B board, and headphones must be unplugged.

Decoder Audio: 3 kHz or 6 kHz v2.46-r5

The FT8/FT4 and Digital pages listen to the radio's own sound. Decoder Audio sets how much of that sound they take in:

  • 3 kHz fast (the default): the sound up to 3 kHz. That holds everything FT8, FT4, RTTY, NAVTEX, PSK31 and WEFAX use, and it is half the work for the radio, so the radio and Web Control stay quick. Leave it here.
  • 6 kHz full: the sound up to 6 kHz, as versions before v2.46-r5 took in. The radio works twice as hard, and with Web Control open the browser can get slower. Choose it only if you get fewer decodes than you expect, and compare the two on the same band for a few minutes each.

To change it:

  • On the radio: Menu → Ham & SWL → Dec. Audio, before you open the FT8/FT4 or Digital page.
  • In Web Control: the Decoder audio list on the Decodes tab (it is also under Settings → Receiver). There the change applies at once, even while the page is open on the radio; the waterfall and the slot start again.

The Decodes tab shows which one is in use (3 kHz audio or 6 kHz audio) beside the mode. The choice is kept when the radio is switched off. The CW decoder is not affected.

Scan, Auto Scan and Seek v2.45-r3

Three items in Menu → Tuning look for stations. Their names are alike and they do different things: Scan shows you the band, Auto Scan goes through the band for you, and Seek takes you to the next station. (Auto Scan was called Scanner until R4.)

ScanAuto ScanSeek

What happensThe radio measures 200 points around your frequency once, with the sound off, comes back and draws the graph under the frequency: 2 MHz wide, 20 MHz on FM. Auto Scan stops on a signal at the mode's squelch level if one is set, otherwise at an SNR of 6 dB or more. In SSB and CW it holds while the station is heard and moves on four seconds after it goes. On AM and FM broadcasts, which never go quiet, it stays five seconds on each station; in FM it skips the next 200 kHz so a strong station does not stop it twice. The step is the tuning step, at least 1 kHz in SSB, 500 Hz in CW and 100 kHz in FM. Set a squelch level first if it stops on noise too often.

Seek uses the receiver chip's own search in AM and FM. In SSB and CW (from R4) it searches the band 1 kHz at a time and stops on the first signal, at most 200 kHz per turn; if it finds nothing it says so and goes back to where it started. A short press in Seek puts a clock in its box: every turn then goes to the next frequency on which the EiBi schedule lists a station at this hour. That needs the schedule loaded and the clock set, and it is not there on FM.

Frequency lock and CPU speed v2.45-r3

Menu → Tuning → Freq Lock → On: the encoder sets the volume instead of tuning, and press-and-turn digit tuning is off. A padlock after the band shows it; it is kept across restarts.

Menu → System → CPU (called Performance before v2.45-r4): 80 MHz (as the official firmware, longest battery), 160 MHz or 240 MHz. Faster makes the screen and menus quicker and uses more battery; the battery estimate shows the difference. The FT8 and Digital pages always run at 240 MHz while they are open, and from v2.46-r5 so does the radio while a browser has Web Control open, so that the page answers quickly; your setting applies again when the page is closed. The Status page shows the speed you set.

Knob speed-up: Enc. Accel. v2.46-r5

The knob can count a quick turn as more than one step per click, so that you cross a band with a flick of the wrist and still stop exactly with slow clicks. Enc. Accel. (encoder acceleration) switches this on or off.

SettingWhat the knob does
On (the default)Turned slowly: one step per click. Turned faster: each click counts as 2, 4, 8 and, at full speed, 16 steps. Slow down and it is one step per click again.
OffAlways one step per click, however fast you turn.

A step is whatever you are turning at the time: the tuning step on the main screen (1 kHz, say), one unit of Volume, Brightness, Squelch, Calibration, Sleep Timer or Off Timer, or one line of the Memory list. Lists of choices and the menu itself always move one item per click, with either setting.

  • Leave it On if you tune across wide ranges: the ALL band from 150 kHz to 30 MHz, the FM band, a shortwave broadcast band.
  • Choose Off if the frequency jumps further than you meant when your hand moves quickly; if you go through a band channel by channel and count the clicks (9 kHz channels on medium wave, 5 kHz on shortwave); or if you fine-tune CW and SSB in 10 Hz steps and want every click to be the same.

To change it:

  • On the radio: Menu → System → Enc. Accel., the tile with the fast-forward arrows on the last System page. Turn to choose, click to keep.
  • In Web Control: Settings → Power and time → Knob speeds up when turned quickly.
  • On the Config web page: Encoder Acceleration.

All three are the same setting, and it is kept when the radio is switched off. It is not the same as Menu → System → Knob (20 or 30 clicks: the kind of knob fitted) or Menu → Display → Scroll Dir. (which way the menu moves).

Antenna capacitor on medium wave: Capacitor v2.46-r5

Inside the receiver chip, at the antenna input, there is a small tuning capacitor for medium wave. The radio can keep it at one fixed value, or let the chip adjust it by itself every time you tune, to draw the most signal from the antenna. Capacitor chooses between the two.

SettingWhat the radio does
Off (the default)The capacitor stays at one fixed value. Right for almost every radio.
OnThe chip adjusts the capacitor by itself each time the frequency changes.

Where it acts: only on the bands MW1, MW2, MW3 and 160M, that is from 150 kHz to 3.5 MHz. On FM, on the shortwave bands and on the ALL band nothing changes, whichever you choose.

Which radios it helps: only a radio without the Hi-Z circuit, a small input stage added between the whip antenna and the chip. Most ATS Mini radios sold now have that circuit, and on them this setting does nothing useful; that is why it is off. If you do not know which radio you have, leave it Off, or try it:

  1. In the evening, tune a weak station on MW2 and look at the signal strength on the S-meter.
  2. Menu → Tuning → Capacitor → On and click. The radio tunes the station again.
  3. Compare. Stronger or clearer: keep On. The same or weaker: go back to Off.

Trying it harms nothing. With On, changing band or mode in SSB on these four bands takes a fraction of a second longer.

To change it:

  • On the radio: Menu → Tuning → Capacitor, the tile with the tuning fork on the last Tuning page.
  • In Web Control: Settings → Receiver → Automatic antenna capacitor.
  • On the Config web page: Automatic Antenna Capacitor.

All three are the same setting, and it is kept when the radio is switched off.

Wi-Fi on the radio v2.45-r4

Menu → System → Wi-Fi is one page for everything Wi-Fi. The top says where to find the radio's web pages: its IP address on your network (or atsmini.local), or 10.1.1.1 after joining the radio's own network ATS-Mini. Below are three rows:

  • Mode: Off, AP Only (the radio's own network), AP+Connect, Connect, or Sync Only (sets the clock at power-on, then switches Wi-Fi off). Click, turn, click to apply; a press cancels.
  • Join a network: the networks in range, strongest first, with signal, security and channel. Pick one and type the password with the knob on the on-screen keyboard (aA for a capital, #+= for symbols, Del, Show). OK lights up at 8 characters. The network is saved and the radio connects at once (AP+Connect) and shows its IP address.
  • Power: Normal (17 dBm), Low (13 dBm) or Lowest (8.5 dBm), for boards that restart when Wi-Fi transmits.

The radio has 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi only: 5 GHz networks are not listed. Networks can still be added from the radio's web page as well.

Web Control v2.45-r4

On the same network as the radio, open http://<radio IP>/control on a phone, tablet or computer (or the Control link on the radio's web pages). The top always shows the frequency, band, mode, step, filter, S-meter, battery (volts and percent), the memory you are on, and the RDS station name and text. Changes apply to the radio at once and are saved as from its own menu. While a browser has Web Control open the radio runs its processor at full speed, so the page answers quickly; this uses a little more battery, and the CPU setting applies again when the browser leaves (v2.46-r5). Beside the station name the page shows the radio's clock: UTC and, when the radio's time zone is not UTC, Local; Clock not set until the radio has the time (v2.46-r5).

  • Receiver: step arrows, Seek (in SSB and CW too), a frequency box (kHz; MHz on FM), mode buttons (Stereo or Mono shown on FM), Band, Step, Filter and AGC lists, volume, a button per band, and the band scope: Sweep measures 41 points around the frequency; click a bar to tune there.
  • Memory: tune to a memory, save the current station, delete one.
  • Beacons: a world map of the 18 NCDXF/IARU beacons. The five on the air pulse in their band's colour. Tap a frequency to tune there in CW: each 10 second slot, the radio colours the beacon it heard (green well, yellow weakly, grey not heard). After one 3 minute cycle you see where the band is open to. Tap a station to follow it across the bands.
  • Decodes (v2.46-r5): starts and stops the decoders on the radio and shows what they decode; see Decodes in the browser.
  • Settings: every setting of the radio's menus, in groups. Bluetooth, USB and Wi-Fi modes and firmware updates stay on the radio.
  • More: the radio's own screen with its knob and buttons (for its menus and the FT8 and CW pages), and Listen (experimental): the radio's sound in the browser on V4B boards, phone quality, best in Chrome. Headphones plugged into the radio cut this sound, and the decoders' input, on V4B boards.

Updates

With Wi-Fi connected, Menu → System → Update FW → Check looks for a newer RESONIK release on GitHub, and Update installs it. Settings are kept. The radio's Config web page has Backup and restore (v2.46-r5): one file with all settings, bands, memories and Wi-Fi networks, to keep or to put on another radio. From v2.46-r5 the radio also looks by itself, a few minutes after joining Wi-Fi and then once a day, and shows a page when there is a new release: Update now, Later or Skip this version. A small arrow beside the Wi-Fi icon, and a banner on the radio's web pages, show that an update is waiting. Menu → System → Upd. Notice → Off stops it. New versions are also announced on this site and on GitHub.

The radio's first web page, Status, shows from v2.46-r5 how long the radio has been running, why it last restarted (switched on, update, crash, watchdog, low voltage), the processor's temperature and the speed you set, free memory and the Wi-Fi signal, each with a word that says whether it is good, fair or bad, in green, yellow or red. If the radio ever restarts by itself, read Last restart there before switching it off, and send it with your report. The backup file holds your Wi-Fi passwords: keep it private.

To return to the official firmware, install it by USB as described on the install section, using the official files.
