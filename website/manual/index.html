Using the RESONIK additions

This page covers what RESONIK adds. Everything else — bands, modes, memories, seek and scan, EiBi schedules, RDS, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth — works as in the official firmware and is described in the ats-mini user manual.

The basics: rotate the encoder to tune. A click (under half a second) opens the Menu and selects; a short press (over half a second) opens the volume, or does the item's second action; a long press (two seconds) turns the display off and on. Menu paths on this page are for the tile menu, the default from v2.45-r2; see The menu for where each item sits, and for the classic menu.

CW mode

Choose Menu → Tuning → Mode → CW (it follows AM). The display shows the frequency of the CW signal itself, the way a CW transceiver does: tune until the frequency matches a spot or a schedule and the tone is in the middle of the filter.

BandwidthToneUse
0.5k (default)500 HzCrowded bands, weak signals
1.0k1000 HzFinding signals, faster tuning
1.2k and wider700 HzListening around the band

The step starts at 10 Hz; change it in Menu → Tuning → Step. CW uses the USB calibration value of the band (Menu → Ham & SWL → Calibration).

Band plan

On the amateur bands from 160 to 10 m, a coloured strip under the tuning scale shows the IARU Region 1 band plan, and the segment you are in is named next to the pointer.

ColourSegment
CyanCW only
YellowNarrow modes: CW and digital (FT8, PSK, RTTY…)
RedInternational beacons (NCDXF/IARU)
GreenAll modes, including SSB phone
GreySatellite and FM (10 m)

Menu → Ham & SWL → Band Plan:

  • Off — no strip.
  • Scale — the strip and segment name only.
  • Scale+SSB (default) — also selects the usual sideband when you tune into a phone segment in LSB or USB: LSB below 10 MHz, USB above. A short message confirms it. If you then change the sideband yourself, your choice stays until you leave the segment.

The plan is Region 1 (Europe, Africa, the Middle East). National allocations differ; check your own licence before transmitting with another radio.

Time zone and dual clock

Menu → System → Time Zone lists UTC first, then every UTC offset with named places beside it — Dubai and Muscat next to UTC+4, London, Berlin/Paris, New York, Sydney and others. Named places change to summer time and back automatically. A previous offset setting is carried over.

When the zone is not UTC, the time line in the info box alternates every few seconds between local date and time and UTC: time — the time used by schedules, logs and beacon timetables. Time: --:-- means the clock is not set yet.

Menu → System → Date/Time shows the running time in UTC. Rotate to change a value, click for the next field, and short-press (over half a second) to save. Leaving without a change leaves the clock alone.

Setting the clock

The radio has no clock battery, so the time is lost when it is switched off. To have it set automatically at every power-on:

  1. Add your Wi-Fi network (see the ats-mini manual, Wi-Fi).
  2. Set Menu → System → Wi-Fi → Sync Only. The radio connects, sets the time and switches Wi-Fi off again to save battery. (Connect mode also sets the time, and stays connected.)

Messages: Time synchronized (internet time server), Time set from web (the network blocks time servers; the time was read from a web server instead), or Time sync failed with a second line giving the reason — show it to whoever looks after your network. Without Wi-Fi, an FM station sending RDS time also sets the clock, or use the web settings page: Use Browser Date/Time copies your phone's or computer's time and time zone.

Battery time

On battery, the voltage at the top right alternates with an estimate of the listening time left, for example ~8h20. The ~ means a typical consumption is assumed; after about ten minutes the radio uses its own measured drain and the ~ disappears. Screen brightness, volume and Wi-Fi all change the result. On USB power the charging symbol is shown instead.

Automatic attenuation

In AM with Menu → Audio → AGC/ATTN set to On, a very strong signal — a local medium-wave station, a big broadcaster at night — can overload the receiver and cause distortion and stations that should not be there. The radio now adds attenuation in steps when that happens and removes it again on weaker stations. The info box shows Att: A12 while it is active (A for automatic). Turn it off in Menu → Audio → Auto Att., or set AGC/ATTN by hand, which always takes over.

The menu v2.45-r2

A click opens the menu on six categories of large tiles. Turn the encoder to move the highlight and click to open a category. Inside, the first tile is Back; a category with more than five items has a second page (the dots at the bottom show which page you are on). A short press closes the menu from anywhere.

Listening controls such as Band, Mode, Step or Volume open their list as before; after your choice the menu closes, so the encoder tunes straight away. Settings open full screen as a list of values, or a number with a bar; a click keeps the value and returns to the category.

Menu → System → Knob: 20 clicks or 30 clicks per turn, for the two kinds of encoder fitted to these radios. If the knob skips a step, or needs two clicks for one, choose the other value (the web page calls it Half-step Encoder).

Menu → Display → Menu Style → Classic brings back the official list menu: listening controls under Menu, the rest under Menu → Settings, with the same names. Tiles switches back.

Beacons v2.45-r2

Eighteen beacons of the NCDXF/IARU International Beacon Project take turns on 14100, 18110, 21150, 24930 and 28200 kHz, ten seconds each, and repeat every three minutes. Each sends its callsign in CW and then four dashes at 100 W, 10 W, 1 W and 0.1 W, so how many dashes you hear tells you how good the path is.

Tune to one of the five frequencies (CW or USB): the station line shows the beacon on the air now and the seconds left, for example Beacon 4X6TU Israel 6s. The clock must be set.

Menu → Ham & SWL → Beacons and a callsign makes the radio follow that beacon: it changes band every ten seconds as the beacon moves from 20 m to 10 m, and waits while it is silent. Tuning by hand, or choosing Off, ends it.

Memory scan v2.45-r2

Menu → Memory → Mem Scan steps through the stored memories (at least two) and holds on any with a signal; it carries on four seconds after the signal goes. A signal means the mode's squelch level if you have set one, otherwise an SNR of 6 dB or more. Rotate the encoder or click to stop: the radio stays on that memory.

Signal logger v2.45-r2

Menu → Memory → Logger writes a line every 10 seconds, minute or five minutes: UTC time, frequency, mode, band, signal strength (dBµV), SNR (dB) and the station name. Leave the radio on a frequency overnight to see when a path opens and closes.

Download the file from the radio's web page, Log (CSV), and open it in a spreadsheet. Clear log in the same menu (click to confirm) deletes it. Logging stops by itself at 1 MB.

Propagation v2.45-r2

Menu → Ham & SWL → Propagation shows the solar flux (SFI), sunspot number, A and K index, X-ray level and the day and night conditions for 80–40, 30–20, 17–15 and 12–10 m, as published by N0NBH on hamqsl.com. The data is fetched whenever Wi-Fi is up — including the time Sync at power-on — and at most every 15 minutes. Higher SFI and low K mean better conditions on the higher bands.

EiBi schedules v2.45-r2

Menu → Ham & SWL → Load EiBi now loads the season's schedule with its days of the week, so a station that is on air only at weekends is shown only then, and adds the language and target area after the name, for example BBC World Service [E > ME] (English, to the Middle East). After updating from v2.45-r1, load the schedule once more.

CAT control v2.45-r2

Menu → System → USB Port → CAT makes the radio answer Kenwood TS-480 commands on its USB port, so programs can read and set frequency and mode and read the S-meter. In the program choose Kenwood TS-480 (Hamlib rig 2028) on the radio's serial port; the speed setting does not matter. Transmit commands are refused: this is a receiver. Switch back to Ad hoc for the ats-mini remote tools and screenshots.

Updates

With Wi-Fi connected, Menu → System → Update FW → Check looks for a newer RESONIK release on GitHub, and Update installs it. Settings are kept. New versions are also announced on this site and on GitHub.

To return to the official firmware, install it by USB as described on the install section, using the official files.
